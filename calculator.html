1. The 3-Step Mental Shortcut

  • Start from the Right: Label the rightmost payload digit ×3, the next left ×1, alternating 3, 1, 3, 1...
  • Group & Multiply: Sum all ×3 digits and multiply by 3 once; then add all ×1 digits.
  • Round Up to 10: The gap (0–9) to the next multiple of 10 is your check digit.

2. Why Alternate 3 and 1?

A plain sum catches single-digit typos, but cannot catch swapped neighbors (47 vs 74). Alternating weights (3 and 1) catches 100% of single-digit errors and ~89% of adjacent transpositions.

3. Universal GS1 Formula

Every GS1 key (GTIN-8, 12, 13, 14, GLN-13 & SSCC-18) uses the exact same equation:

CD = (10 − (Sum mod 10)) mod 10

Pro Tip: Because weights anchor from the right, an 18-digit SSCC (17 payload digits) starts with ×3 on position 1, whereas a 13-digit EAN/GLN (12 payload digits) starts with ×1 on position 1. Switch the GS1 tabs above to watch the colors flip!

5. GS1 Check Digit & GTIN Conversion FAQ

How do you calculate a GS1 check digit for GTIN, EAN, UPC, GLN, or SSCC?

Start from the rightmost digit before the check digit and assign alternating weights of 3 and 1 moving right to left. Sum all weighted products and find the distance to the next multiple of 10 using Check Digit = (10 − (Sum mod 10)) mod 10.

How do you convert a 12-digit UPC-A to EAN-13 or GTIN-14?

Prepend one leading zero (0) to a 12-digit UPC-A to form an EAN-13 / GTIN-13, or prepend two leading zeros (00) to form a base-unit GTIN-14. For carton or case-level GTIN-14 codes using packaging indicators 1 through 8, replace the first digit with the indicator and recalculate the final Modulo 10 check digit.

Does converting UPC-A to EAN-13 or GTIN-14 change the check digit?

Adding leading zeros (indicator 0) never changes the check digit because zero multiplied by any weight is zero. However, if you assign a non-zero GTIN-14 packaging indicator (1 to 8) for a case or master carton, the check digit changes and must be recalculated.

How do you calculate a GLN-13 check digit?

A Global Location Number (GLN-13) uses the exact same 12-digit payload and Modulo 10 weighting as GTIN-13 / EAN-13. Take the first 12 digits, multiply alternating positions by 3 and 1 from right to left, sum the products, and subtract from the next multiple of 10.

Can you convert a GTIN-14 or EAN-13 back to a 12-digit UPC-A?

Yes — whenever an EAN-13 starts with 0 or a GTIN-14 starts with 00, stripping those leading zeros yields the exact 12-digit UPC-A with the same check digit. If a GTIN-14 starts with a packaging indicator 1–8 followed by 0, remove the first two digits and recalculate the check digit for the 11-digit UPC payload.
