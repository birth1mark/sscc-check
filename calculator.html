How to Learn GS1 Modulo 10 in 30 Seconds

Every GS1 key (GTIN-8, GTIN-12, GTIN-13, GTIN-14, GLN-13, SSCC-18) ends with a single check digit calculated by the exact same universal rule — regardless of length.

The 3-Step Mental Shortcut:

  • 1. Start from the Right: Label the rightmost payload digit ×3, the next left ×1, alternating 3, 1, 3, 1... all the way to the start.
  • 2. Group & Multiply: Add all ×3 digits together and multiply by 3 once; then add all ×1 digits.
  • 3. Round Up to 10: Ask "how much do I need to add to reach the next number ending in 0?" That gap (0–9) is your check digit!

Why Alternate 3 and 1?

A simple sum catches single-digit typos, but cannot catch swapped neighbors (47 vs 74 have the same sum!). Multiplying neighbors by different weights (3 and 1) catches 100% of single-digit errors and ~89% of adjacent transpositions (except pairs differing by 5, like 16↔61, where 5×(3-1)=10).

Formula:

CD = (10 − (Sum mod 10)) mod 10

Pro Tip: Because weights anchor from the right, an 18-digit SSCC (17 payload digits) starts with ×3 on position 1, whereas a 13-digit EAN (12 payload digits) starts with ×1 on position 1. Try switching the tabs on the left to watch the colors flip!
